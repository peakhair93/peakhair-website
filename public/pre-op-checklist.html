The countdown

Three stages to a smoother procedure day

These are organizational checkpoints—not clinical stop/start dates. Confirm all medical timelines with your own clinician, particularly if you travel or take prescription medicines.

01When surgery is planned

Review the full plan

Confirm the treating clinician, procedure location, proposed technique and what is included in your written quote. Share your medical conditions, allergies, prior procedures and a complete list of prescription, over-the-counter, hair-loss and topical medicines and supplements through your clinic's approved channel. Ask whether smoking, nicotine use or any other habit changes the plan.

02Before the date approaches

Settle medicines and logistics

Ask your clinician which medicines and supplements to continue, change or hold—and when. This includes blood thinners, pain medicines, diabetes treatments, finasteride, minoxidil and herbal products if you use them. Do not stop prescribed treatment on your own. Confirm travel, time away from work, transport home if sedated, and how to contact the clinical team afterward.

03The day before and day of

Follow your personalized directions

Recheck your arrival time, procedure address, payment arrangements and clinician's instructions for food, fluids, medicines and hair washing. Do not shave or dye your hair unless instructed. Choose comfortable clothing that does not need to be pulled over the scalp afterward. If fever, a new illness, scalp irritation or a medication change occurs, call the treating team before arriving.

Important: Never follow another clinic's fixed schedule for stopping medication, caffeine, alcohol, nicotine or food. Requirements depend on your health and the type of anesthesia and procedure. Ask the clinician caring for you for instructions in writing.

Before-surgery questions

What should I ask?

Should I stop minoxidil, finasteride or a blood thinner?

Do not stop or change a medicine based on this website. Tell your treating clinician every medicine and supplement you use; they will tell you what applies to your case. If another doctor prescribed a blood thinner, coordinate with that doctor as directed by your surgical team.

Should I shave my head before the procedure?

Only if your surgical team specifically instructs you to. The extent of trimming can depend on the chosen technique and donor plan.

Do I need to fast or eat breakfast?

Your clinical team's instructions for food and fluids depend on the planned anesthesia and your medical needs. Ask them for a written answer rather than assuming that a standard breakfast—or fasting—is right for you.

What should I wear and bring?

Comfortable, front-opening clothing may help avoid contact with the scalp afterward. Confirm what identification, paperwork, medicine list and payment method your clinic needs, and whether someone must accompany you home.

What if I get sick right before surgery?

Contact the treating clinical team promptly if you develop fever, illness or a new scalp issue. They decide if the procedure can safely proceed.

What happens after surgery?

Ask for your personal discharge instructions and follow-up contacts. For general educational context, read Peak's post-op guide; your clinician's written directions take priority.
