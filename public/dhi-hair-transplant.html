The short answer

What is DHI, and how is it different from FUE?

“DHI” commonly means placing harvested hair grafts with an implanter device rather than putting them into recipient sites prepared in a separate step. FUE describes one way of harvesting those grafts from a donor area; DHI describes an approach to placing them. The two can be used together. The implanter does not create extra donor hair or guarantee denser growth. Ask your surgeon exactly how Peak would harvest and place grafts in your case.

The International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS) explains FUE harvesting and the importance of precise recipient-site placement.

Your treatment overview

DHI hair restoration at a glance

A useful treatment proposal explains more than the instrument used. These details should be agreed after assessment, with the responsible clinician and procedure arrangements identified before you book.

What DHI describes

Implanter-assisted graft placement, commonly using a sharp implanter to create a site and place a graft in the same action.

Donor harvesting

Often FUE, with individual follicular units removed from an assessed donor area. The implantation pen is not the harvesting tool.

Graft count

Personalized to donor capacity and treatment priorities. A graft is a follicular unit and can contain more than one hair.

Shaving

Full, partial or unshaven preparation may be discussed. Donor and recipient preparation are separate clinical decisions.

Procedure length

Depends on the graft plan, medical considerations and workflow. Confirm the anticipated duration and whether staging is recommended.

Cost and follow-up

A written quote should identify what treatment, reviews and aftercare are included. Ask about optional extras separately.

The practical distinction

What an implanter changes

The placement instrument may allow the clinician to create a site and insert a graft in one maneuver. That does not remove the need for safe harvesting, careful graft handling or a long-term hairline plan.

Donor planning stays the same

A clinician must assess how many grafts can safely be taken and what reserve may be needed as untreated hair changes.

Placement is the focus

Angle, depth and direction matter for a natural appearance whether grafts are placed with an implanter or another method.

Names are not outcomes

DHI is not proof of scar-free surgery, no shedding, faster recovery or guaranteed density. Individual healing and growth vary.

A closer look at the procedure

Your DHI treatment plan, step by step

The sequence and responsibilities must be explained by the treating team. This overview describes the questions to resolve, not a standardized Peak protocol or a promise that every patient follows the same plan.

Medical and scalp assessment

Review the diagnosis, pattern and progression of thinning, medical history and previous treatment. New or unexplained shedding may need investigation before surgical planning. Explain your goals and what has changed recently.

Donor review and hairline design

Assess usable donor supply and agree restoration priorities. Discuss how the proposed outline suits your features and future hair loss. Ask how grafts would be distributed rather than focusing only on a total count.

Preparation and comfort

Confirm which areas would be trimmed, the anticipated procedure length and the anesthesia plan. Hair transplantation commonly uses local anesthesia; your clinician assesses medical suitability and explains the approach.

Harvesting and graft protection

With FUE harvesting, follicular units are removed individually. Careful handling, appropriate holding conditions and coordination remain important before placement. DHI does not bypass the harvesting stage.

Loading and implantation

Grafts are loaded into an implanter and placed according to the recipient plan. With a sharp implanter, site creation and insertion occur together. Ask how graft size, depth, angle and direction are managed in your case.

Aftercare and review

Receive written instructions, the agreed review schedule and the treating clinic's contact pathway. Confirm what to do if healing is unexpected and how growth will be assessed over the following months.

Different regions, different priorities

DHI for hairline restoration, temples and crown

A placement instrument can be considered for different scalp regions, but it does not determine which area should be treated first. Your diagnosis, existing hair and donor reserve set the boundaries.

Receding hairline

The leading edge needs an appropriate shape and transition into the hair behind it. Discuss hairline height, follicular-unit selection and growth direction. A lower outline may use donor hair that could be needed elsewhere later.

Temple recession

Temple restoration needs to fit the surrounding hair and facial proportions. Explain whether your priority is softening the corners or rebuilding a broader outline. Ask what can be achieved without overcommitting the donor area.

Mid-scalp coverage

Where native hair remains, the clinician reviews its condition and the likelihood of future thinning. Placement must fit a long-term plan rather than simply fill every visible gap at the consultation.

Crown hair restoration

The natural swirl and the area requiring coverage affect distribution. Ask how crown treatment competes with frontal priorities for the same donor supply, and whether staging would be preferable.

Hairline redesign

Bring earlier photographs of your own hairline and discuss your goals. The plan should suit your current features and expected progression—not reproduce someone else's photograph or promise original density.

Previous transplant concerns

Existing graft direction, scars and remaining donor supply can limit further treatment. An implanter alone cannot guarantee correction of an earlier result. Bring records and ask for an assessment of the specific concern.

Individual assessment

DHI considerations for different patients

Men with pattern hair loss

The pace of recession, donor stability and existing treatment help determine whether surgery is appropriate now. Rapid progression may change the recommendation. An attractive hairline today should not leave insufficient supply for the scalp behind it later.

Women with thinning hair

Localized recession and diffuse thinning require different assessment. A clinician should establish the cause and evaluate donor stability before recommending transplantation. A desire to retain hair length does not establish surgical suitability.

Curly and Afro-textured hair

Hair characteristics influence harvesting, handling and planning. Ask about the treating clinician's relevant experience and how donor suitability is assessed. An implanter is not a blanket reason to recommend or exclude treatment for a particular hair type.

Read our Afro-textured hair transplant information for additional consultation questions.

Uncertain or active hair loss

Patchy loss, scalp symptoms or diffuse donor thinning may need further investigation. The appropriate next step can be diagnosis, medical management or monitoring rather than transplantation. DHI placement does not treat every cause of hair loss.

Hair length and visibility

Can you have DHI without shaving?

Possibly, depending on the clinical plan. An unshaven recipient area may be considered separately from donor preparation. Some plans use limited donor trimming beneath longer hair; others require more extensive shaving. Ask exactly what would be cut and why.

Long-hair transplantation can also refer to retaining length on the graft shafts themselves. That is not the same as leaving surrounding hair long. Review our unshaven hair transplant Manhattan page to understand these distinctions.

Discreet does not mean invisible

Existing hair may help cover parts of the treated area, but redness, crusting, swelling or a newly restored hairline can remain visible. Do not plan an important event around a promise that nobody will notice surgery.

Discuss work duties, headwear and washing before agreeing to a date. Preserving length must remain compatible with access, careful placement and postoperative graft protection. A pen does not automatically eliminate shaving or shorten healing.

Recovery, risks and the written plan

Plan for healing, not just procedure day

An implanter changes how grafts are placed; it does not remove the need to protect a healing scalp. Ask your surgeon to explain your donor-site care and recipient-site care separately.

The first two weeks

The NHS advises particular care with new grafts during this period. Confirm washing, sleep position, work, exercise and headwear restrictions in writing. DHI is not a promise of immediate return to every activity.

Shedding and regrowth

The AAD describes transplanted hairs shedding two to eight weeks after surgery, with many patients seeing results at six to nine months and some at twelve months. Your own course may differ; an early photo cannot establish the final result.

Risks to discuss

Bleeding, infection, anesthetic reactions, visible scarring and poor growth can occur with hair transplantation. Hair direction, placement depth and donor preservation also matter. Ask how problems would be assessed and who you would contact.

Compare the whole plan, not the pen

Request the proposed graft range, harvesting method, hairline design, clinical roles and follow-up schedule. If you are comparing DHI with Sapphire FUE, ask what actually changes in those steps. A higher fee or a particular instrument is not evidence of a better outcome for you.

Evaluate evidence carefully

DHI before-and-after results

Ask whether a photographed case used implanters alone or a combination of placement methods. A generic hair transplant photograph should not be described as a DHI result without documentation of the technique used.

Compare the initial pattern of loss, donor characteristics, treatment areas and time since surgery. Similar lighting, styling and hair length make photographs more informative; a frontal image alone cannot establish crown coverage or donor condition.

What images cannot promise

Another patient's appearance does not predict your graft growth, healing or final density. An immediate photograph shows placement, not mature growth. Discuss what your proposed treatment can realistically achieve and what is likely to remain thin.

Future hair loss still matters

What does direct hair implantation mean?

Does the Choi pen extract the donor hair?

How many grafts can I have with DHI?

There is no universal safe number. The treatment area, usable donor supply, medical suitability and workflow determine the proposed range. A fixed package count cannot replace an assessment.

Can DHI restore my crown and hairline together?

Possibly, if the donor supply and clinical plan support both. Ask how follicles would be divided and whether concentrating on one region or staging treatment better fits your priorities.

Does DHI create higher density?

An implanter alone cannot guarantee higher density. Hair characteristics, graft distribution, recipient planning and donor limits all affect the appearance that can realistically be achieved.

Is DHI a surgery without incisions?

No. Sharp implanters create recipient incisions during placement, and harvesting also involves surgery. Combining site creation and placement does not make the treatment incision-free.

Is DHI painful?

Local anesthesia is commonly used for hair transplantation, but injections and postoperative tenderness may be uncomfortable. Ask your clinician to explain the anesthesia and pain-management plan.

Can women have DHI implantation?

Potentially, where the diagnosis and donor stability support transplantation. Diffuse thinning or an active scalp condition may change the recommendation. The implantation label does not establish candidacy.

Can DHI correct a previous transplant?

A review is needed to assess the specific problem, existing grafts, scars and remaining supply. An implanter cannot guarantee correction of every previous result or make depleted donor hair return.

How long does a DHI procedure take?

When can I return to my Manhattan office?

Will transplanted hair shed before growing?

Can I combine DHI with Sapphire FUE?

Is DHI better than Sapphire FUE?

They describe different steps: DHI refers to implanter-assisted placement; “sapphire” refers to a blade used to create recipient sites. Neither term alone predicts a better result. Ask how each proposed method fits your graft and hairline plan.

Does DHI need a shaved donor area?

That depends on the harvesting approach and the number and location of grafts. DHI describes placement, not whether the donor area is shaved. Confirm the exact preparation during consultation.

The placement stage explained

What does a DHI implanter actually do?

Harvesting and implantation are separate decisions

A hair transplant requires donor harvesting and recipient placement. FUE describes the individual removal of follicular units from a donor area. DHI commonly refers to placing grafts using an implanter device. Describing a case as DHI therefore does not tell you everything about donor harvesting, hair preparation or the design of the result.

An implanter is loaded with a graft and used to place it at the planned location. Sharp implanters can make an incision during placement; dull implanters place grafts into sites prepared beforehand. The ISHRS describes both approaches. Ask which is proposed and who is responsible for site creation, loading and placement. A named pen cannot guarantee graft survival or compensate for unsuitable donor hair.

DHI vs Sapphire FUE: compare equivalent steps

Sapphire describes a blade for recipient-site preparation, while DHI describes an implantation approach. Rather than asking which term is universally better, ask what changes in your particular workflow. How will recipient sites be planned? How will the angle and direction match existing hair? What happens to harvested grafts before they are placed?

The same questions apply to forceps placement and other implantation methods. The goal is careful handling and a design appropriate to your hair, not choosing a device before your assessment. Use the Sapphire FUE page and complete FUE techniques guide to compare the terminology without confusing harvesting with placement.

Suitability before technique

Can DHI work around existing hair?

Existing hair does not remove the need for planning

Some patients want additional coverage between existing hairs rather than rebuilding a bare area. Your clinician needs to assess the diagnosis, hair distribution and whether existing follicles are continuing to thin. The plan should address what happens if native hair is lost later, not only what the area looks like immediately after graft placement.

Ask whether medical management, monitoring or surgery is appropriate now. An implanter is not a treatment for every cause of shedding. New patchy loss, scalp discomfort or an uncertain diagnosis may require investigation first. A clear recommendation should explain both the reason to proceed and the findings that would make the clinician advise waiting.

Does DHI mean an unshaven transplant?

Not necessarily. The donor area and recipient area can be prepared differently. A patient may keep some surrounding hair long while selected donor follicles are trimmed; another plan may involve a more extensive shave. Long-haired grafts are a separate technical consideration. Ask for a description of each area rather than assuming the DHI label promises no shaving.

If your priority is discretion at work, discuss the haircut and visible healing separately. Redness, crusts or donor changes may still be noticeable. Longer surrounding hair does not eliminate the need for careful washing and graft protection. The ISHRS guide to shaving terminology explains why these distinctions matter.

Density, graft numbers and future coverage

Density depends on the treatment area, hairs within each graft, hair characteristics and the safe distribution of donor units. A pen does not generate extra follicles. Ask how many grafts are proposed for each region, what remains available and whether a staged approach could be preferable. If a clinic quotes a fixed graft number before assessing your donor area, request the clinical basis before agreeing.
