Stem cell hair therapy

“Stem cell therapy” can mean very different products and procedures. Before considering one for hair loss, find out exactly what is being offered, where it is provided, and what evidence and regulatory status apply. This page does not claim proven regrowth or FDA approval.

The short answer

What does “stem cell hair therapy” mean?

It is an umbrella term, not one standardized hair-loss treatment. Clinics may use it for procedures involving a patient's own tissue, processed cells, or cell-derived products. Those approaches are not interchangeable with platelet-rich plasma (PRP). The ingredients, processing, administration, risks and legal status matter far more than the label. Ask Peak for the exact product and procedure before deciding.

The US Food and Drug Administration warns that stem-cell and exosome products marketed for many conditions may be unapproved and can pose serious risks. Its approved stem-cell products are for certain blood disorders, not hair loss.

Evidence and suitability

What can it actually do?

Early hair-loss studies of regenerative approaches are promising in some cases, but use different products and small samples. A systematic review advises caution until findings are repeated in larger, more representative studies. No result for a specific Peak procedure can be inferred from those studies without knowing its formulation and protocol.

A clinician should first diagnose the type of hair loss and explain established alternatives. Neither stem-cell terminology nor an injection is a substitute for that assessment. Do not assume this treatment is suitable after a transplant or will improve graft survival.

What to ask before you consent

  • What exactly is the product? Is it live cells, tissue, cell-derived material or something else?
  • Where does it come from and how is it processed?
  • At which clinic and under which clinician is it available?
  • What is its regulatory status in that location for hair loss?
  • Which clinical studies used the same product and protocol?
  • What risks, alternatives, total price and follow-up apply?
US patients: Do not assume a product is FDA-approved for hair restoration. Ask for the product name and documentation before booking; the FDA says there are no FDA-approved exosome products and its approved stem-cell products are not approved for hair loss.
Common questions

Before you decide

Is this the same as PRP?

No. PRP is prepared from a blood sample and concentrates platelets. “Stem cell” may refer to entirely different materials and procedures. Read Peak's PRP page to compare questions worth asking.

Is stem cell hair therapy FDA-approved?

Do not assume so. According to the FDA, currently approved stem-cell products are for blood-forming disorders, not hair restoration. Confirm the exact product and whether it is authorized for the intended use and location.

Does it guarantee thicker hair or better transplant results?

No. There is no reliable guarantee of regrowth, density or graft survival. Ask what evidence applies to the actual product proposed for you.
