The short answer

What is a beard transplant?

A beard transplant is an FUE procedure that moves individual follicles into thin or patchy areas of the beard, cheeks, goatee or mustache. The International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS) describes the back and sides of the scalp as the main donor area, with hair from under the jawline as a second option when fewer grafts are needed.

Gaps or low density on the cheeks, chin or jawline that have been there since your beard came in.

A fuller mustache or goatee, or a connection between the two.

Choose the change that matters to you

Patchy beard, mustache and goatee restoration

Small gaps and a broader beard outline can require very different donor commitments. Describe the appearance you want at your usual grooming length, then discuss which changes are realistic.

Cheeks and sideburn connections

Filling a gap is different from moving the upper cheek line. Ask how the new hairs will follow the changing growth directions and blend into the sideburns. A soft transition can matter more than a sharply drawn border.

Mustache transplant

The upper lip needs a plan suited to its existing growth and your preferred shape. Discuss the central area, outer edges and any scar separately. Scalp donor hair may need different grooming from your natural mustache.

Chin and goatee

Connecting the mustache to the chin or filling a sparse central area should account for direction and skin contour. Ask how the result may look as short stubble as well as when grown longer.

Jawline and lower border

A permanent outline should fit your face beyond a current style trend. Review the proposed border before surgery and ask what would remain visible if you later shave closely.

Stable scar coverage

Selected scars may be assessed for transplantation, but tissue condition and blood supply can affect suitability and growth. A scar's appearance alone cannot predict graft survival or establish that it is ready for surgery.

A broader beard outline

Building several regions can require a substantial donor allocation. Ask whether a focused first stage or a more conservative design better protects your options for later scalp restoration.

A new bald patch needs diagnosis first. Sudden or changing facial-hair loss, itching, active acne or inflamed skin can call for medical assessment rather than graft placement. Tell the clinician when the change began and about any skin treatment you use.

How it works

Your beard transplant step by step

  1. Design. Your clinician maps the beard shape with you and agrees the areas to fill.
  2. Extraction. Follicles are taken one by one from the donor area, usually the back of the scalp.
  3. Placement. Each graft is placed at the angle and direction of natural facial hair, which usually grows at a very flat angle to the skin.
  4. Recovery. Small crusts form around the grafts in the first days. You follow the aftercare plan your clinician gives you.
  5. Growth. Transplanted hairs commonly shed in the first weeks, then regrow over the following months.
Worth knowing

Donor hair is limited

The ISHRS notes that donor hair is an unrenewable resource. If you may also want a scalp transplant in the future, your clinician should plan both together.

For darker skin, the ISHRS advises taking any beard donor grafts from under the jawline rather than the face, because extraction marks can leave lighter spots.

Design, recovery and limits

A beard that fits your face and future

A beard transplant is a lasting design decision. Discuss how the outline will look if you grow your beard long, trim it short or later choose to shave. Your donor hair and skin need assessment before a proposed graft count means much.

Assess skin before surgery

Active acne, infection or inflammation may need treatment before grafts are placed. New or unexplained beard patches need diagnosis rather than an automatic transplant recommendation. Scarred areas require an individual assessment.

Match direction and texture

Grafts need to follow the flat angles and changing directions of facial hair. Ask to review relevant cases and discuss how scalp donor hair may differ from your existing beard. Dense packing alone cannot create a natural appearance.

Budget for donor supply

Hair used for the beard is no longer available for a future scalp procedure. Your clinician should consider both goals together. Some patients may need more than one procedure to approach the agreed coverage.

Recovery is not immediate fullness

Avoid pressure and friction on new grafts, and follow your clinician's directions for washing, shaving and sun exposure. Transplanted hairs may shed before regrowth. ISHRS describes regrowth beginning around three to four months and overall growth assessed around ten to fourteen months; these are general timelines, not a guaranteed schedule.

Ask for a clinical contact and review plan before leaving the clinic. See our general post-op guide; beard-specific instructions from your treating team take priority.

Risks beyond temporary redness

Possible facial-transplant complications include follicle inflammation or infection, pigment changes, bumps around grafts and hair growing at an unwanted angle. Donor extraction can leave visible small scars. Discuss your skin history, likely cosmetic trade-offs and what could be done if growth or appearance falls short.

Source: ISHRS beard and mustache transplant guidance. This describes general evidence, not Peak-specific outcomes. Seek prompt clinical advice for severe pain or unexpected symptoms.

The size of the recipient area, donor source, existing hair, scar tissue and procedure arrangements can affect the plan. Filling a localized mustache gap is not equivalent to creating a wider beard outline. Ask how these differences affect your own proposal.

A lower cost per graft is not automatically better value if the design uses more donor hair than you need. Likewise, a higher price does not prove better growth or a more natural appearance.

Before your procedure

Prepare your skin, schedule and questions

Bring a history of your facial-hair growth, previous surgery, skin conditions and treatments. Share any tendency toward raised scars or pigment changes, plus all medicines and supplements. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without agreement from the relevant prescribing clinician and surgical team.

Ask whether your existing beard and donor hair should be trimmed, and leave preparation changes to the treating team's instructions. Discuss shaving irritation, acne treatments and skin-care products before choosing a date.

The pre-op checklist can help organize the conversation. Your clinical team's beard-specific directions take priority.

Plan for visible healing

Do not assume that a small treatment area means an unnoticed recovery. Redness, crusting and swelling may affect your appearance. Customer-facing work and jobs involving tight masks, chin straps or helmets need a separate discussion about pressure on the treated skin.

Agree a personal grooming plan

Confirm when you may wash, trim, shave and return to your usual skin-care routine. A razor, electric shaver and scissors do not interact with healing skin in the same way. Avoid picking crusts or using products to conceal healing without clinical guidance.

Know who to contact

Keep the treating clinic's contact details and review schedule. Ask how severe pain, unexpected swelling, persistent bleeding or signs of infection should be assessed, including outside regular hours.

Common questions

Beard transplant in NYC: your questions

How many grafts will my beard need?

The area to fill, existing growth, hair thickness and available donor supply determine the proposal. There is no universal graft count for a full beard or a patch. Ask for a distribution by region and realistic coverage limits.

Will scalp hair turn into beard hair?

Transplanted scalp hair can retain characteristics of its original location. It may differ in texture or growth from surrounding facial hair and need regular grooming. Discuss matching before choosing the donor source.

Does a beard transplant leave scars?

FUE leaves small extraction scars, and facial placement can cause visible bumps, pigment changes or other skin changes. The result is not scar-free. Ask how these risks might look with your preferred hairstyle and beard length.

Is beard implantation painful?

Local anesthesia is commonly used, but injections and postoperative tenderness can be uncomfortable. Your clinician should explain the comfort plan and any relevant medical risks rather than promise a painless procedure.

Can DHI or sapphire tools improve beard results?

Implanters and recipient-site instruments are parts of a workflow, not guarantees of better facial-hair growth. Ask which method is proposed, who performs each step and why it suits the region being treated.

Can I combine scalp and beard restoration?

Both may draw on the same donor reserve. An assessment must consider your priorities, treatment extent and likely future loss before deciding whether combined or staged care is appropriate.

Will I need another beard transplant?

Some patients consider more than one procedure to approach their goals, but further treatment is not automatically suitable. The first result, remaining supply and skin condition must be reviewed, and additional costs explained.

Do the photos below show beard transplant results?

No. The existing comparison gallery shows Sapphire FUE scalp cases and is labelled accordingly. Ask the treating clinician for documented beard or mustache cases relevant to your goals; scalp photographs cannot predict facial outcomes.

Who is a good candidate for a beard transplant?

People with patchy or low-density facial hair, gaps in the beard or mustache, or scars from acne, burns, injuries or surgery may be candidates, according to the ISHRS. A clinician examines your face and donor area to confirm.

Is the transplanted beard hair permanent?

Transplanted follicles usually come from scalp areas that are resistant to balding, so they tend to keep growing. A clinician will explain what to expect for your own hair.

How long until I see results?

Transplanted hairs commonly shed in the first weeks, then regrow over the following months. Your clinician will give you a timeline for your case.

Can I shave after a beard transplant?

Your clinician will tell you when it is safe to trim and shave. Once grown in, the transplanted hair can usually be trimmed and shaped like the rest of your beard.

A facial-hair transplant explained

Beard FUE: where the follicles come from

Donor harvesting and facial placement

A beard transplant relocates follicles rather than creating new ones. Donor hair is commonly obtained from the scalp. FUE removes individual follicular units, while strip harvesting is another donor method described in medical guidance. These methods have different scar patterns, and the choice needs a clinical assessment rather than a universal recommendation.

Facial placement can use forceps or implanters. A recipient-site instrument or placement label does not determine suitability on its own. The direction, angle and distribution of grafts matter because facial hair often lies close to the skin and changes direction across the cheeks, chin and mustache. The ISHRS beard and mustache guide describes these considerations and their limits.

Donor texture and future scalp needs

Scalp hair may differ from your existing beard in texture, growth behavior and grooming requirements. Ask how that difference could look at your preferred beard length. A suitable plan should account for the surrounding facial hair rather than assuming the same graft choice works everywhere.

Using scalp follicles for a beard reduces the donor supply available for later scalp restoration. If you also have a receding hairline or family history of progressive thinning, discuss both goals before committing to a large facial procedure. This is especially important if a full beard outline would require more donor hair than a small patch correction. Explore FUE techniques and donor planning for the wider surgical context.

Choose the outline carefully

Patchy cheeks, mustache gaps and beard shape

Design for the way you actually wear your beard

Discuss whether you want fuller cheeks, stronger sideburn connections, a mustache change or a more continuous goatee. Those requests involve different recipient areas. Your clinician should explain the proposed outline and where natural transitions will be placed, including how it may look if you later shave or change styles.

A sharply drawn border is not automatically the best permanent design. Ask how the proposed graft direction will blend with your existing growth. Bring examples to communicate preferences, but do not assume a reference beard can be copied with your donor supply. Graft numbers should follow the assessed area and your realistic coverage goal.

When a patch needs diagnosis rather than grafts

A new patch of facial-hair loss should not automatically lead to surgery. Your clinician needs to examine the skin and history to decide whether an underlying condition needs treatment. Active infection, inflammation or acne can affect planning. Stable scars may be considered individually, but the appearance of a scar alone cannot establish how grafts would grow.

Tell the clinician about past scars, pigment changes, previous facial procedures and any ongoing skin treatment. Explain whether your main concern is density, shape or a specific patch. These details help separate a cosmetic design request from a medical problem that needs investigation first.

Understand the growth and grooming commitment

Transplanted shafts can shed after surgery before new growth appears. ISHRS describes regrowth beginning around three to four months and overall growth being assessed around ten to fourteen months, with individual variation. Early appearance should not be mistaken for a final result. Ask when the team will assess growth, direction and any skin changes.

Get beard-specific instructions for washing, shaving and sun exposure. Do not assume scalp-transplant instructions answer every facial-care question. If more than one procedure might be needed, ask how that decision would be made and what further cost or donor use could be involved.
