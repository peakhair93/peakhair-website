After your hair transplant, one day at a time.

A clear guide to protecting grafts, washing, sleeping, daily activity and the months of growth ahead. Keep the written instructions from your treating clinician close: those instructions always take priority over a general website guide.

Your first priority

Follow your own clinical plan.

Care instructions vary by procedure and patient. Confirm your wash date, products, medicines, activity limits and follow-up contact before you leave the clinic.

For unexpected symptoms, contact your treating clinical team. For urgent symptoms or if you cannot reach them, seek local urgent medical care.

The phases

What recovery can look like

These are broad milestones, not a schedule for your individual case. Graft care is especially important in the first two weeks; your surgeon determines when each restriction can change.

01Procedure day

Get home and protect the area

Review the written instructions and emergency contact with your treating team. Keep the grafted area from being rubbed or knocked. Use prescribed medicines and dressings exactly as directed. Have someone help if you were given sedating medication.

02First few days

Gentle care, no guesswork

Mild tenderness, swelling or small crusts can occur. Sleep in the position your clinician recommends to avoid pressure on the grafts. Ask before wearing anything that touches the recipient area. Start spraying or washing only on the date and with the method you were given.

03First two weeks

Let the scalp heal

The NHS advises extra care with transplanted hair during this period because grafts may not yet be secure. Do not pick at scabs or scrub the new hairline. Follow your team's wash and return-to-work instructions; avoid strenuous activity until they clear it.

04Following weeks

Changes in appearance are common

Some transplanted hairs may shed before new growth appears. This can be expected, but any unusual symptom deserves a check. Ask the treating team when it is safe to resume exercise, swimming, helmets, haircuts and products rather than following a generic calendar.

05Following months

Track progress, not individual days

Visible changes take time and differ between people. Keep your scheduled clinical follow-ups and any photo check-ins your team requests. The surrounding hair may continue to thin, so discuss any concerns about the long-term plan with your clinician.

Before you leave the clinic: Ask for written instructions for your own wash date and method, sleeping position, medicines, activity, sun exposure, follow-up schedule and the phone number for medical concerns. Peak's general scheduling email is not an emergency channel.
Everyday practicalities

Four things to plan for

01 · Washing

Be gentle with grafts

Use only the water pressure, shampoo and washing technique in your clinician's instructions. Do not scratch, rub or remove crusts. If the written plan is unclear, call the clinical team before your first wash.

02 · Sleeping

Avoid pressure and friction

Follow the sleep position and pillow guidance you were given. The goal is to prevent your grafted area rubbing against bedding, especially early in healing. Ask your team when your usual position is safe again.

03 · Activity and outdoors

Return gradually

Your clinician may advise less exercise during early healing. Check before heavy exercise, swimming, saunas, direct sun or headwear that could press on grafts. Do not assume that feeling well means the scalp has healed.

04 · Medication and follow-up

Use your written plan

Take only the medicines and topical products your clinician recommends, exactly as directed. Keep the follow-up appointment even if recovery seems straightforward; contact the team if something changes unexpectedly.

Safety first

What is expected, and what needs a call?

Recovery differs from patient to patient. When in doubt, contact the practice that performed your procedure rather than trying to diagnose a problem from a photo online.

Often part of healing

  • Some temporary tenderness, mild swelling and crusting.
  • Transplanted hair shedding before later regrowth.
  • Uneven appearance while hair and skin settle.

These are not reasons to ignore worsening or unexpected symptoms.

Contact your clinical team promptly

  • Severe or worsening pain, swelling, redness or bleeding.
  • Fever, feeling unwell or discharge from the scalp.
  • Any unexpected symptom, medicine reaction or injury to the grafted area.

If symptoms are urgent or the clinical team cannot be reached, seek urgent medical care locally.

The small questions

Answers to check against your care plan

When do I wash my hair?

Your treating team should give you a written start date, shampoo and technique. Timing differs by procedure; do not copy another clinic's schedule or rub the grafts.

How should I sleep?

Sleep in the position your clinical team recommends, avoiding pressure or friction on the grafted area. Ask how long that restriction applies to you.

Can I wear a hat or helmet?

Ask your clinician before placing anything against the transplanted area. The answer depends on fit, pressure and how the scalp is healing.

When can I exercise, swim or get a haircut?

There is no universal date that suits every procedure. Check the restrictions in your written instructions and get clearance before resuming activities that cause sweating, friction or scalp exposure.

Is shedding a sign the transplant failed?

Shedding of transplanted hair can happen during recovery and does not by itself prove failure. Your clinician can assess your pattern and expected progress at follow-up.

Whom do I contact about a complication?

Contact the licensed clinical team at JC Jimenez Medical Health Wellness Services PLLC or the treating clinic named on your discharge paperwork. Peak's coordination team can help with scheduling, but does not give medical advice. Seek urgent medical attention for urgent symptoms.

Questions about your follow-up?

Peak coordinates scheduling and patient communication. Your treating clinician answers medical questions and determines your individual aftercare instructions.
