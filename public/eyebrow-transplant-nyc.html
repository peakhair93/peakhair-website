The short answer

What is an eyebrow hair transplant?

An eyebrow transplant relocates hair follicles, usually from the scalp, into selected areas of the brow. It can be considered for partial filling or more extensive reconstruction. Unlike pigment applied by makeup or microblading, a transplant places follicles that may grow hair.

The International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery describes donor selection, individual graft placement and a shape agreed between patient and physician. Scalp donor hair can continue growing longer than natural eyebrow hair, so regular trimming and styling may be needed.

Lasting does not mean maintenance-free. Growth, direction and density vary. Some patients consider an additional procedure, and not every graft survives. Ask what ongoing grooming your proposed donor hair is likely to require.
Diagnosis before design

Who may consider eyebrow restoration?

Over-plucked or naturally sparse brows

Long-standing gaps or a naturally thin outline may prompt an assessment. Discuss how long the area has looked this way and whether loss is still changing. A photograph alone cannot determine whether follicles are permanently absent.

Patchy growth or uneven shape

A focused plan may address a particular section rather than both brows in full. Your clinician reviews existing direction and the natural differences between the two sides. Perfect symmetry is not a realistic guarantee.

Selected stable scars

Previous injury, burns or surgery can leave areas without hair. Scar maturity, tissue condition and blood supply affect suitability. A visible scar is not proof that grafts will grow normally or that transplantation is the right first step.

Reconstruction after hair loss

Loss related to illness or medical treatment needs its own clinical review. Conditions causing ongoing loss should be addressed before surgery is considered. Active hair pulling also needs appropriate management rather than simply replacing the removed hair.

New or unexplained eyebrow loss needs investigation. Tell the clinician about rapid changes, itching, skin inflammation and other hair loss. An assessment may lead to dermatology care, medical treatment or monitoring instead of transplantation.
Framing your own features

Eyebrow shape, direction and donor matching

Agree the outline before proceeding

Review the inner brow, body, arch and tail together. Bring older photographs if you want to discuss your previous appearance. A fashion reference can explain your preferences, but another person's brow may not suit your facial proportions or available donor hair.

Consider how you wear your brows with and without makeup. A fuller outline is a long-term design choice, not an easily reversible pencil line. Ask to view the proposed shape from different angles before agreeing to the procedure.

Hair should follow the brow, not stand away from it

Eyebrow growth direction changes across a small area, and the hairs lie close to the skin. The clinician considers angle, orientation, natural curl and the use of fine grafts. Misdirection, curling or hairs projecting away from the skin are possible cosmetic complications.

Matching donor texture matters

The finest suitable donor hair is not identical in every patient. Ask how thickness, curl and color will blend with the brow hair you still have. The transplant does not automatically turn coarse scalp hair into fine eyebrow hair or remove the need for grooming.

From assessment to placement

Your eyebrow transplant procedure, step by step

1. Review the cause of loss

The treating clinician reviews your medical and skin history, previous cosmetic procedures, current medicines and goals. Share prior brow tattooing or microblading, scarring concerns and any ongoing change in growth.

2. Plan the donor supply and brow design

The assessment establishes which hair may be appropriate, what areas to fill and the proposed graft range. There is no universal count for an arch, tail or full brow. Grafts and individual hairs are different measures; ask what each number in your proposal means.

3. Confirm preparation and comfort

Discuss donor trimming, anesthesia, the anticipated procedure duration and who performs each stage. The plan depends on your clinical needs. Do not assume a sedation approach or a fixed appointment length from a general webpage.

4. Harvest and place the grafts

With FUE harvesting, follicles are removed individually from the assessed donor area. Grafts are prepared and placed to follow the agreed eyebrow outline. FUE describes harvesting; an implantation device is a separate choice and cannot guarantee growth.

5. Leave with a written care plan

Confirm cleaning, sleeping, grooming restrictions and clinical review arrangements. Ask how to contact the treating practice if a concern develops, including outside ordinary hours. The general website inquiry form is not an emergency service.

Healing and everyday care

Eyebrow transplant recovery and growth

Expect visible healing, not instant fullness

Small crusts, swelling, redness, bruising or tenderness can occur. Do not rub, scratch or pick the treated brows. Your team should explain how to clean the region and protect it without disturbing the grafts. Return-to-work planning depends on your duties and how comfortable you feel with visible healing.

Hair may shed before regrowth

The immediate appearance reflects placed hairs and healing, not mature density. Some transplanted hair may shed before new growth emerges over the following months. Agree review points with the clinician rather than applying a guaranteed date or a universal growth percentage to your case.

Trimming becomes part of the routine

Scalp donor hair may grow longer than the eyebrow hairs around it. Regular trimming and, once cleared, styling to help hairs lie in the intended direction may be needed. Ask the clinical team to demonstrate safe grooming before trying new products near healing skin.

Makeup, microblading and skin care

Get individual clearance before using brow pencils, tint, lamination, topical products or additional pigment procedures. Existing microblading should be discussed during design; implanted hair will not remove pigment or automatically cover an unwanted tattoo shape.

Read the general postoperative guide alongside your eyebrow-specific instructions. Contact the treating practice promptly for severe pain, unexpected symptoms or concerns near the eye.

An informed decision

Risks and limits to discuss

  • Growth may fall short: not every graft grows, and the achieved density can differ from the plan.
  • Direction can be difficult: curling, misdirected hairs or hairs standing away from the skin may require ongoing grooming or further assessment.
  • Skin and donor changes: scarring, pigment changes, altered sensation, pain, swelling, bruising, infection or follicle inflammation can occur. FUE leaves small extraction scars.
  • Another procedure may be considered: suitability, timing, donor supply and any additional fee require a new discussion—not an automatic promise of a touch-up.

Ask how your skin history and any existing scarring change the assessment. A technique name, a low graft count or a small treatment area does not remove surgical risk.

Eyebrow restoration: your questions

Is an eyebrow transplant different from microblading?

Yes. A transplant moves follicles that may grow hair. Microblading places pigment in the skin to create a cosmetic effect; it does not implant follicles. The suitability, upkeep and risks differ, and neither option is right for everyone.

Do transplanted eyebrows need trimming?

Usually, ongoing trimming is needed because scalp donor hair can retain its longer growth characteristics. Frequency depends on your hair and preferred style. Follow your clinician's advice on when to begin grooming.

Can men and women have eyebrow transplantation?

Both can request assessment. Diagnosis, skin condition, donor matching and expectations—not gender alone—determine whether transplantation is appropriate.

Can you transplant into previously microbladed eyebrows?

Previous pigment does not automatically rule treatment in or out. Tell the clinician what was applied and when, including removal attempts. Skin condition and the relationship between the pigment and desired hair outline need review.

Is DHI or Sapphire FUE required for eyebrows?

No label establishes suitability or a superior result. FUE concerns donor harvesting, Sapphire describes a recipient-site instrument and DHI commonly describes implanter-assisted placement. Ask about the actual method and the clinician's relevant experience.

Will eyebrow transplantation also restore eyelashes?

No. Eyelash transplantation is a separate, highly specialized procedure. This page addresses eyebrows and does not advertise eyelash surgery.

Can I see eyebrow before-and-after photographs?

Request documented eyebrow cases from the treating clinician, including healed close-up views and follow-up timing. Peak's general scalp gallery is not evidence of eyebrow outcomes, so no scalp comparisons are presented here as eyebrow results.

Are the results permanent?

Transplanted follicles may provide long-lasting growth, but survival, density and direction are not guaranteed. Underlying disease can affect the area, and ongoing grooming may be needed. Discuss your diagnosis and long-term expectations before deciding.
